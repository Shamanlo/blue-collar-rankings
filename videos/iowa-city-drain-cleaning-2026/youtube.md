What moves your price: how bad the clog is, where it is and how easy it is to reach, the method (hand snake, cable machine, camera or hydro jetting), and timing. Many companies charge 1.5x to 2x after hours.

DIY vs. pro: a plunger ($5 – $15) or hand auger ($20 – $50) can handle shallow clogs. Chemical drain cleaners ($8 – $25) are hard on older pipes. Grease and roots need a cable machine or hydro jetting, and a camera shows what's in a line that keeps clogging.

Signs a drain needs cleaning: slow drains, a sink that fills while the dishwasher runs, a gurgling toilet or tub, a sewer smell near a floor drain, water coming up during a storm, or a clog that's back a week after plunging. One slow fixture usually means a local clog; several at once usually means the main line.
