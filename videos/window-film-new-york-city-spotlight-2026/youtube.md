Most New Yorkers cannot replace their windows. In a co-op or condo the windows often belong to the building, replacement needs board approval and an architect, and in a landmarked building the Landmarks Preservation Commission may have a say about anything that changes the facade.

Window film can block up to 99% of UV rays, up to 80% of solar heat and up to 90% of glare (typical upper ranges; your numbers depend on the film and the glass).

Window film for New York City homes:
• Decorative: Etched, patterned and textured films for brownstone doors
• Security: Thick safety film that holds shattered glass together
• Glare Reduction: Reduce screen glare and squinting from river views
• Energy-Saving: Cuts the solar gain that drives summer cooling

Commercial window film:
• Solar Control: Solar control film for commercial buildings with large glass exposures
• Conference Room Privacy: Frosted and patterned film for glass-walled conference rooms and offices
• Low-E: Low-emissivity film that reflects room heat back inside in winter
• Office: Glare control, heat rejection and privacy for Manhattan

Prewar Co-ops, Brownstones and Glass Condo Towers: The housing stock here ranges from 1900s walk-ups with wood double-hung sashes to 2010s condo towers with floor-to-ceiling insulated glass. Those are very different jobs.

How an install works: send rough window sizes through the quote form, see film samples on your own glass, installation day (a typical house in a day or less), then a short curing period.

Common questions:
• Is window film allowed on rental apartments? Many landlords allow it because quality film can be removed cleanly.
• Can window film be installed on floor-to-ceiling glass in condo towers? Yes, but large insulated glass units need a compatible film.
• How long does window film last in New York? Interior residential and commercial films typically last many years when installed on the inside surface and cleaned gently.
