Houston's cooling season is long. Air conditioners start working hard in spring and often do not get a real break until fall, and the humidity means nights stay warm enough that the house never fully sheds the day's heat.

Window film can block up to 99% of UV rays, up to 80% of solar heat and up to 90% of glare (typical upper ranges; your numbers depend on the film and the glass).

Window film for Houston homes:
• UV Protection: Blocks nearly all ultraviolet light to slow fading on hardwood floors
• Privacy: Daytime privacy for bathrooms
• Decorative: Etched, striped and gradient patterns for front-door sidelights
• Security: Thick safety film that holds broken glass in place

Commercial window film:
• Anti-Graffiti: A clear sacrificial layer over storefront and lobby glass
• Decorative & Frosted Glass: Frosted bands, logos and patterned film for office partitions
• Solar Control: Heat and glare rejection for large commercial glass
• Conference Room Privacy: Frosted or patterned film on meeting room glass

Houston Deed Restrictions, HOAs and Historic Districts: Houston has no zoning code, but that does not mean anything goes. Deed restrictions cover many older neighborhoods, and master-planned communities in the suburbs have HOAs with architectural review committees that care about how a house looks from the street.

How an install works: send rough window sizes through the quote form, see film samples on your own glass, installation day (a typical house in a day or less), then a short curing period.

Common questions:
• Does window film protect against hurricanes? Safety film holds broken glass together and reduces flying shards, but it is not hurricane-rated impact protection unless it is part of a tested system.
• What is the lifespan of window film on a Houston house? Interior film on residential glass typically holds up for many years, often longer than a decade, and the film maker's warranty spells out the exact term.
• Can adding film affect the warranty on my windows? It can.
