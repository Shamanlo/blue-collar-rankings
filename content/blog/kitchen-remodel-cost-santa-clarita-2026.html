Entry-level remodel: $25,000 to $35,000

This tier keeps your existing layout and appliances and focuses on surfaces. It suits small to midsize kitchens and typically includes:

  • Cabinet refacing or new stock cabinets
  • Laminate or quartz countertops
  • New lighting, backsplash, paint and flooring
  • Basic plumbing and electrical updates

Mid-range remodel: $40,000 to $70,000

This is where the kitchen starts working differently, not just looking different. Expect:

  • New semi-custom cabinets
  • Quartz, granite or butcher block countertops
  • An updated layout, with plumbing or electrical relocations
  • New sink, faucet, lighting and tile backsplash
  • Higher-quality flooring and appliance upgrades

A useful data point from the guide: one Santa Clarita homeowner wasn't sure a $40,000 budget would cover the layout change they wanted. It did, with smart design, semi-custom cabinetry and value-conscious selections.

High-end remodel: $75,000 to $120,000+

A full redesign, often moving or removing walls. This tier can include:

  • Layout redesign and wall reconfiguration
  • Custom cabinetry with organizers
  • Full-slab quartz or granite countertops
  • Commercial-grade appliances
  • Premium tile or hardwood flooring and high-end lighting design
  • Ventilation improvements or structural changes
  • A custom island, bar or built-in seating

What drives the price up or down

The same kitchen can land in very different tiers depending on these choices:

  • Kitchen size and layout, and whether the layout changes
  • Cabinet quality: refaced, stock, semi-custom or custom
  • Countertop material
  • Appliance upgrades
  • Plumbing or electrical work
  • Flooring and lighting
  • Permits and inspections

Popular extras that add cost: panel-ready appliances, custom storage like pantry pull-outs and appliance garages, large-format or bookmatched slab backsplashes, heated floors, beverage or coffee stations, new windows, doors or skylights, and smart kitchen tech.

Before you hire

  • Confirm the contractor's license with the California Contractors State License Board.
  • Ask who pulls the permits, and whether permit fees are in the quote.
  • Ask for clear allowances for cabinets, counters and fixtures, so you know what's included before you pick finishes.
  • Get the price and timeline in writing, and compare two or three quotes.
